01 — the shop as it is

Six machines.Six towers. And the cable between them.

Every control needs its own tower, its own serial card and its own run down the aisle. The longest here is 21 m. Move a machine and it gets longer.

02 — six separate points of failure

Nothing here is sharedexcept the problems

A dead tower stops one machine. A long run drops a transfer part-way. Each one is its own repair, its own walk across the floor, its own version of the file.

03 — what comes out

Five towers go.So does the floor cable.

One Elecio mounts at each control. What remains of the serial run is a short jumper that stays connected, per machine — and one tower stays, on the bench.

04 — the link

One send,every control at once

The tower that stayed pushes the program over the shop network. Each unit takes it at its own machine, at the same time, with nothing running between them along the floor.

05 — what replaces them

One PC left.Handling all six.

Programs go from a single place to any control on the floor. The far corner of the shop is the same send as the machine next to the bench.

06 — when the network drops

The link goes down.The machines do not.

Three controls lose the station. Each unit already holds the program in its own memory, so the spindles keep turning and the file keeps feeding into the control on its own.

07 — and it comes back

The link returnson its own

The three come back without anyone walking out to them, take what they missed, and report run time and idle time to the same screen as the rest.
